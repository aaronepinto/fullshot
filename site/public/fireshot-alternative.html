FireShot alternative: screencappy

Last verified: August 15, 2026

FireShot is the most capable full page capture tool in this comparison, and also the most expensive. If you want reliable whole page screenshots with annotation included at no cost, screencappy is the simpler trade.

FireShot Pro costs $39.95 per year or $99.95 once. screencappy is free, open source under Apache-2.0, and does everything it does on every platform.

What does FireShot do well?

Format coverage and browser coverage, both of which are genuinely best in class here.

It exports PDF with live links and searchable text, plus PNG, JPEG, GIF and BMP, and it runs on Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Opera, Safari and a long list of others. It also holds a 4.8 rating from 51.6K ratings on the Chrome Web Store as of today, which is not an accident.

What does FireShot cost?

FireShot Pro is $39.95 per year, or $99.95 as a one time lifetime licence. There is a seven day money back period.

One thing to know before you compare prices: the $39.95 figure is advertised as a limited time 60% promotion, and the undiscounted price is not published anywhere on the site.

What does the free version of FireShot leave out?

More than most people expect. Editing is entirely absent from the free tier.

By the vendor's own comparison table, FireShot Lite also has no multipage PDF, no custom page size, no printing, no clipboard or email output, and no upload destinations. Lite history is limited to the last week, and Lite is the only tier marked as carrying advertisements.

Is FireShot's annotation available on Mac and Linux?

Mostly not, even if you pay. This is the detail that catches people out.

The yearly plan's own feature bullet reads "Editor & smart annotations (on Windows)", and in FireShot's comparison table the non Windows Pro column has a tick on only one row in the whole Editing section. screencappy's annotation tools are the same on every platform Chrome runs on.

Is FireShot open source?

No. The EULA reserves all rights and prohibits modification, decompilation and reverse engineering, and there is no public source repository anywhere on the site.

Does FireShot upload my screenshots?

No, and it is clear about it. The vendor states that captures are processed locally in the browser and are not uploaded to external servers, and that it works with the internet off.

The upload features that exist are Pro only and user initiated, sending to third party services or to your own FTP or HTTP server. As with several tools in this category, FireShot's privacy policy is dated 2019 and does not mention screenshots at all, so the assurance sits in product copy rather than in the policy.

Which one should you use?

If you need Safari or Firefox, or you need PDFs with searchable text and live links, FireShot is the more complete tool and worth its price.

If you are on Chrome, and what you actually want is capture, annotate, export, without a subscription and without an editor that only works on Windows, screencappy covers that for free.
